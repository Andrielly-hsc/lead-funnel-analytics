# Near-duplicate leads: choosing a matching rule

> Generated by `lead-funnel profile` from the warehouse and the generator's injection log.
> 500 near-duplicate pairs were injected: the same person registered again under a new
> `lead_id`, with the e-mail sometimes in upper case, the phone in another format
> (`+34 156 …` vs `0034-156-…`) and the company name lower-cased or without legal suffix.

| Strategy | Predicted pairs | True matches | False matches | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| exact e-mail (as typed) | 265 | 265 | 0 | 1.000 | 0.530 | 0.693 |
| normalised e-mail | 500 | 500 | 0 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

| fuzzy company name ≥ 100 (same country, ≤ 14 days) | 6,804 | 500 | 6,304 | 0.073 | 1.000 | 0.137 |

- **Precision** = share of predicted pairs that are real duplicates (1 − false-match rate).
- **Recall** = share of real duplicates that were found.

## Which error is worse?

| Error | Consequence | Who is harmed |
|---|---|---|
| **False match** (two people merged) | conversions and revenue attributed to the wrong lead; one customer's contact history or data shown to another | the customers (GDPR accuracy and confidentiality, art. 5(1)(d) and (f)), and trust in the CRM |
| **Missed match** (one person twice) | double counting in funnel metrics; the person may be contacted twice | mostly internal: metric bias, mild annoyance |

A false merge is **harder to detect and to undo** than a missed one, and it can expose
personal data. The rule for *automatic* merging must therefore favour **precision**;
recall can be improved later through human review.

## Decision

1. **Automatic flag** (`is_probable_duplicate` in `fct_leads`): normalised e-mail **or**
   normalised phone, compared as salted hashes in dbt (`int_leads__duplicates`).
   On this data: precision 1.000, recall 1.000.
2. **No automatic merge on company name.** Fuzzy name matching finds few real duplicates and
   many false ones: company names are not unique (many small firms share similar names),
   so similarity says little about the *person*. At most it could feed a review queue.
3. Note what normalisation buys: comparing e-mails exactly as typed finds only 53% of
   the duplicates; lower-casing them finds all of them at no cost in precision.
4. Caveats: on real data a shared e-mail is not always the same person (e.g. `info@`
   mailboxes, shared family phones), so precision would be below 1.0. A labelled sample
   should be reviewed before switching any rule from *flag* to *merge*.
